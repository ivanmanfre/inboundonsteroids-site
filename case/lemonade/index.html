The two-founder problem.

David and Billy run Lemonade together. Both of them post. Both of them sell. The brand has one point of view, but the two of them do not sound remotely alike, and a feed where they blur into one another is a feed nobody believes.

Keeping two real voices apart, on one shared brand, was the thing that had to be solved before anything else was worth running.

Topics arrive

The post goes out on the right founder's account, at his slot, in his name.
The steering rule they use most. One sentence of direction on a topic changes what gets written. Say nothing and the week goes out anyway, as the right man.

Lane 2 · Lead magnets

6 formats, built end to end.

A lead magnet here means the whole thing: the asset itself, the launch post, the email, the page copy, the DM replies and the cover, all delivered together under their brand. Every one exists to earn an email address worth having.

Calculators, checklists, templates

Produced as finished spreadsheets with working formulas, formatting and Lemonade branding. No manual build.

Guides and swipe files

Written, then published as a live resource page on their own domain, cover and capture form included.

Interactive assessments

A self-scoring tool a reader actually completes, which is the version that earns an email address worth having.

When a lead magnet goes live, its launch post goes live with it.

Lane 3 · Capture

The comments turn into a list worth selling to.

When a post asks readers to comment for the asset, everyone who does gets it. Then the interesting half of the job starts.

Everyone gets the asset

No reader is left waiting on a founder to send a link by hand.

Each one is graded

Every commenter is graded against their ICP, with the reasoning written down, so a wrong call can be seen and corrected.

Only fits reach the list

The email list stays made of people they would actually want on a call, instead of everyone who ever typed a word.
