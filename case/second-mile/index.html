01
The problem
01 / 03

The week between yes and started.

An agency's most dangerous week is the one right after a client says yes. Somebody has to create the records, pick the team, build the folders, write the kickoff deck, assign the work. Every step is copy-paste, and every step can quietly not happen.

For an ops agency that sells clean operations, a scrappy onboarding is not just slow. It is off-brand.

02
What runs for them
02 / 03

The deal builds its own onboarding.

One trigger, in their own CRM. Everything below happens on its own, with one human approval in the middle, exactly where judgement belongs.

1
approval between deal won and work assigned
Deal closes
The CRM stage flips to closed won. Nothing else needed from the seller
Record built
The client engagement is created in their PM tool, every field mapped from the deal
Team matched
The right strategist is matched from their own team database, by service line and availability
One approval
The ops lead reviews the prepared engagement and flips one status. This is the whole human step
Kickoff ready
Folders created, kickoff deck generated, tasks assigned, due dates set

Judgement stays human. The busywork does not.

03
The craft
03 / 03

Built around their judgement, not over it.

01
Human in the loop, on purpose
The system deliberately stops once, before anything client-facing exists. Approve is a decision, not a chore, and it is the only one left.
02
Their own team database
Assignments come from a team roster their ops lead controls: service line, active status, capacity. Change the roster, change who gets matched. No hidden rules.
03
Shaped by the people using it
The flow was revised against the team's real feedback in the first weeks, then left to run. It has been running since early 2026.
