Kyle HuntAgency operations coach

A $2.1M creative agency asked me to help them build an AI-powered QA system for their design team. I asked if their designers had a checklist. They didn't.

Their designers kept sending deliverables with wrong fonts, grammar issues, and brand guideline violations. The founders were catching everything in review. They wanted automation to fix it.

...see more

But the real problem was simpler. The designer and the reviewer were checking against two completely different mental standards. Nobody had written down what "done right" actually looked like.

So we built one QA checklist per deliverable type. Then gave the identical checklist to both the creator and the reviewer.

The creator self-checks every item before submitting. The reviewer checks the same items on review. Same standard, same list, no ambiguity.

That self-check step is the part most agencies skip. Without it, every error becomes the reviewer's problem. The reviewer becomes a bottleneck. The founder gets pulled back in to catch things that should never reach them.

I've seen this same structure drop QA needs by 80% in three months at another agency. Strategists got back 10 hours a week. The fix cost nothing.

Agencies want to automate QA before the manual version even exists. You can't automate a process you haven't built yet. A shared checklist is step one. Automation comes after.

Before you assume your team has a quality problem, check whether they have a written standard to check against. If quality only lives in the founder's head, that's not a people problem. That's a process problem.
