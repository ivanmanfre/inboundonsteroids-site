Reach SEO and growth leads measuring brand visibility in AI search.

Written for SEO and content leads who report on AI visibility and want numbers stable enough to act on.

Andy ChadwickKeyword Insights

Your brand has a separate AI ranking for every type of buyer.

I asked ChatGPT for the best marathon shoe twice. Same question, same model. The only thing I changed was one sentence about who I was.

When I said I was a CrossFitter training for a marathon, it picked the Adidas Adizero Evo SL. When I said I was a rock climber training for the London Marathon, it picked the ASICS Novablast 5.

This matters if you report on AI visibility. A lot of prompt tracking asks generic questions on behalf of a customer who doesn't exist. It runs each one once a day and reads the wiggles on the chart as a trend.

To get a number you can act on, you need three things:

  • Prompts written for each real customer segment
  • The same questions every month
  • Several runs per question, so one lucky answer doesn't count as a win

The slides show how to set this up step by step. The last one has a prompt library with two filled-in examples, one for a SaaS tool and one for an online shop, that you can copy and adapt.

If ChatGPT Search checks your own website first, your website needs to answer the buyer's question.

On 8 August, ChatGPT Search started running site: searches at scale. Promptwatch saw those searches go from about 0.4% of background queries to about 17% in a day. Suganthan Mohanadasan found a pattern in the traffic. First comes one search that names the brands. Then comes a site: look-up on each brand's own website for pricing and specs.

Over the next few days, Reddit's share of ChatGPT citations dropped. Google's AI Overviews and AI Mode didn't make the same change, and people still read Reddit.

So keep up the Reddit and third-party work. But also check what a site: look-up would find on your own domain:

  • Is your pricing on a page, written as plain text?
  • Are specs, limits and integrations written out as text on the page?
  • Does each product or plan have its own page with a clear name?
  • Would a stranger read that page as the official source?

If the answer only lives in a sales deck or behind a demo form, the model can't read it when it comes looking.

Prompt library for booking software for dental clinics

01The example business and its three buyers

Say you run ChairTime, booking software for dental clinics. Your two main rivals are SmileDesk and ClinicFlow.

Each persona is one sentence. Paste that sentence before every question, word for word, every month.

  • Practice manager: "I'm the practice manager at a two-chair independent dental clinic and we lose too many appointments to no-shows."
  • Group owner: "I own a group of five dental clinics and need one diary across all sites with reports for each clinic."
  • New dentist: "I'm a newly qualified dentist opening my first practice on a tight budget."

Why three? Each one buys for a different reason, so an AI tool may recommend a different brand to each. Start with these three and add more once the monthly routine is running.

02Practice manager: ten questions from problem to trust

Paste the persona sentence first, then the question. Questions 1 to 3 cover the problem, 4 to 6 the options, 7 and 8 comparison, and 9 and 10 trust.

  1. How can a small dental practice cut down on no-shows?
  2. What is the easiest way to let patients book dental appointments online?
  3. Do dental patients prefer booking online or by phone?
  4. What booking software do small dental practices use in the UK?
  5. Which dental booking systems send SMS reminders automatically?
  6. What is the best online booking tool for a two-chair dental clinic?
  7. ChairTime or SmileDesk for a small independent dental practice?
  8. How hard is it to move our patient diary to a new booking system?
  9. Is ChairTime easy for reception staff to learn?
  10. What do dental practice managers say about ChairTime?

03Group owner: ten questions from problem to trust

Same structure: problem, options, comparison, trust.

  1. How do dental groups manage appointments across several clinics?
  2. How can I see booking numbers for each of my clinics in one place?
  3. What software do multi-site dental groups use for scheduling?
  4. Which dental booking systems handle five or more locations?
  5. What is the best practice management software for a dental group?
  6. Which dental booking systems give reports by clinic and by dentist?
  7. ChairTime or ClinicFlow for a group of five clinics?
  8. Can ChairTime share one patient record across sites?
  9. How do dental groups roll out new booking software across every site?
  10. Is ChairTime reliable enough for a growing dental group?

04New dentist: ten questions from problem to trust

Same structure: problem, options, comparison, trust.

  1. What software do I need to open a new dental practice?
  2. What should a new dental clinic look for in booking software?
  3. What is the cheapest way to take online bookings for a dental practice?
  4. Which dental booking tools have no setup fee?
  5. What is the best booking software for a new practice with one dentist?
  6. SmileDesk or ChairTime for a practice that is just starting out?
  7. Can I start on a basic dental booking plan and upgrade later?
  8. Which dental booking software is easiest to set up alone?
  9. Does ChairTime take card deposits when patients book?
  10. Is ChairTime a good choice for a first dental practice?

05How to run the questions each month

Each month you run 3 personas x 10 questions x 5 runs, which gives you 150 answers.

  1. Open a fresh chat for every run, with memory turned off.
  2. Paste the persona sentence, then the question, exactly as written.
  3. Use the same AI tool and model each month, and write down which one you used.
  4. Run each question five times. One run is one sample, and the next try can name a different leader.
  5. Do the runs in the same week each month so the months compare fairly.

If an answer looks odd, leave the question as it is. The questions stay fixed so that any change you see comes from the model.

06The run log, with example rows

Record one row per answer, with these columns:

  • Month
  • Persona
  • Question number
  • Run number
  • Brands named, in order
  • ChairTime named (yes or no)
  • ChairTime position
  • Pages cited

Example rows from month one:

  • Month 1 | Practice manager | Q6 | Run 1 | SmileDesk, ChairTime, ClinicFlow | Yes | 2 | ChairTime pricing page
  • Month 1 | Practice manager | Q6 | Run 2 | ChairTime, SmileDesk | Yes | 1 | a dental software review site
  • Month 1 | Group owner | Q4 | Run 3 | ClinicFlow, ChairTime | Yes | 2 | ClinicFlow multi-site page
  • Month 1 | New dentist | Q5 | Run 1 | SmileDesk, ClinicFlow | No | none | SmileDesk starter plan page

07Reading the results and deciding what to do

Say month one comes back like this, out of 50 answers per persona:

  • Practice manager: ChairTime named in 40 of 50
  • Group owner: ChairTime named in 15 of 50, ClinicFlow in 45 of 50
  • New dentist: ChairTime named in 0 of 50, SmileDesk in 35 of 50

The blended figure would be 55 of 150. That figure hides the fact that new dentists never hear the name.

Next, read the pages cited in the new dentist answers. In this example they point to SmileDesk's starter plan page and to lists of low-cost tools for new practices. So the work is a plain starter pricing page on your own site, a set-up guide for a one-dentist practice, and outreach to the lists those answers cite.

For the group owner, look at what the ClinicFlow multi-site page says that yours doesn't.

Run the same 150 questions next month and compare the results persona by persona.

Why each prompt starts with a sentence about the buyer

Andy Chadwickto Alex

Hi Alex,

Thanks for picking up the Persona Prompt Library.

There's one detail worth copying exactly. Every question in the library starts with the persona sentence. For example: "I'm the practice manager at a two-chair independent dental clinic and we lose too many appointments to no-shows." Then the question follows.

Most tracking leaves that sentence out. When I asked ChatGPT for the best marathon shoe and changed one sentence about who I was, it recommended a different shoe. The model reads who is asking and changes the list.

So write each persona sentence once, keep it word for word every month, and paste it before every question. If you change the wording, you can't tell whether an answer moved because of the model or because of you.

A simple way to start: pick one persona, write its sentence, and run its ten questions five times each this week. That gives you 50 answers to read, and a baseline for next month.
