The H1 on the page said 'Empowering healthcare organizations to do more.' The site had been live for months and nobody had touched that line.

I audit a lot of healthcare SaaS sites. In most of them, the hero copy was written well enough to launch. The decay happened quietly, in the space between the launch call and the next time anyone on the team opened the CMS with that page in mind.

The H1 is usually where the drift shows up first. Written to sound good in a stakeholder review, approved, pushed live, then left alone while the product added features and the sales team refined its pitch against what buyers actually said on calls.

The team that built the site knew the specifics. The team doing the updates afterward is working from whatever they can read on the page. If the positioning brief is not handed over alongside the CMS credentials, the copy defaults toward generic.

What I now ask every client before we hand off: what is the one phrase your best customer used to describe what you do before they signed? That phrase belongs in the H1. The leadership team's preferred phrasing from the kickoff meeting is usually one abstraction layer too high to mean anything to a buyer comparing you against three competitors.

If the person maintaining your site does not know that phrase, the copy will drift back to something like 'the comprehensive platform for modern healthcare organizations.'

The technical structure of a well-built site can hold for years. The positioning copy needs a named owner and a calendar reminder to re-read it every quarter against what the sales team is actually hearing.
