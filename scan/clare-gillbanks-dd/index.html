Plan the conversation before the event

Written for owners and directors who go to business events and want a simple way to prepare for a 121, ask better questions and follow up with a clear next step.

Clare GillbanksUnlimited Connections - Click & Connect

A good 121 starts before anyone sits down.

Recently I had breakfast with someone from the property and construction world. We kept bumping into each other at London events and kept saying we should sit down properly. When we finally did, ideas for working together came out that a quick hello in a busy room would never have found.

It made me think about how much more a 121 can give when you plan it a little.

Here is how I would set one up:

  1. Write down who you want to meet
  2. Go where those people gather again and again
  3. Ask for the 121 before the event
  4. Take five questions with you
  5. Write the note the same day
  6. Send a message with a next step

I have put all of this into one worked document, with two filled-in examples from different kinds of firms, so you can copy the wording and adapt it to your own business.

Write down who you want to meet

Before you choose an event, describe one person in a sentence: their job, the kind of firm they work in, and the problem you help with.

For example, say a ten person surveying firm writes: "Asset managers at owners of mixed-use buildings in London, who need a surveyor when a lease ends."

A sentence this clear helps you spot the right rooms. It also helps you recognise the right person when they are standing next to you with a drink in their hand.

Does anyone else just not like receiving WhatsApp voice notes?

I know they are quick for the person sending them. For the person receiving them, it can be a different story.

If I am in a meeting or at an event, I cannot listen. If I want to check what was said later, I cannot scan a voice note the way I can scan a written message.

So I use voice dictation instead. It is not always perfect but the other person can read it when it suits them and come back to it later.

If you are messaging someone new, here is a simple way to write it:

  1. Say who you are in one line
  2. Say why you are getting in touch
  3. Ask one question they can answer in a sentence
  4. Offer a specific time, so they only need to say yes

For example: "Hi Sam, I'm Clare, we met at the drinks last week. I'd love to hear more about your new service. Are you free for a coffee on Thursday morning?"

That message can be read in a meeting, found again in a search and answered in ten seconds.

The 121 Brief: two filled-in examples

A surveying firm meeting property asset managers

01The firm in this example

Say Harbour Row Surveyors is a ten person firm in London. It does building surveys and lease-end repair schedules for commercial property. Most of its work comes from referrals, and it would like to meet more of the people who send that work.

02Who to meet, in one sentence

Asset managers at owners of mixed-use buildings in London, who need a surveyor when a lease ends. A second group worth meeting is solicitors who handle commercial leases, because they are often asked to recommend a surveyor.

03Which rooms to choose

Pick two rooms where property people return each month, for example one property networking group and one professional services drinks evening. Go to each three times before you decide if it works. Familiar faces make the third visit much easier than the first.

04The message asking for a 121

"Hi Priya, are you going to the drinks on Wednesday? I'd love to hear what you are working on at the moment. Shall we have a coffee at 4, before it gets busy?"

Book three of these for each event. Even if only one happens, you walk in knowing someone.

05Five questions for the conversation

  1. What are you working on at the moment?
  2. Who would be a useful person for you to meet?
  3. What kind of project is a good fit for your team?
  4. What usually makes a lease-end project harder than it should be?
  5. How do people usually find you?

Write nothing down during the conversation. Listen, then write the note straight afterwards.

06The note, filled in

Priya, asset manager, eight buildings.
Working on: lease-end repair schedules are a headache.
Would like to meet: solicitors who handle commercial leases.
I promised: a two paragraph summary of how we handle those schedules.

The last line becomes your follow-up. The third line gives you a second reason to be in touch.

07The follow-up message

"Hi Priya, lovely to meet you. Here is the summary I mentioned. I also thought of a solicitor you might enjoy meeting. Shall I introduce you? I'm free for a coffee next Thursday if you'd like to carry on the conversation."

How to write the note after a 121

Clare Gillbanksto Alex

Hi,

Thanks for taking a copy of The 121 Brief. Here is one detail from it that is easy to skip and makes the biggest difference: the note you write after the conversation.

Write it the same day, in four lines.

In the surveying example, Harbour Row Surveyors is a ten person firm. After a coffee with Priya, an asset manager who looks after eight buildings, the note reads:

  1. Priya, asset manager, eight buildings.
  2. Working on: lease-end repair schedules are a headache.
  3. Would like to meet: solicitors who handle commercial leases.
  4. I promised: a two paragraph summary of how we handle those schedules.

Line four is the one that matters. It becomes your follow-up message, because it is the thing you told them you would do. Line three gives you a second reason to get in touch, because you might know someone who fits.

The message then writes itself: "Hi Priya, lovely to meet you. Here is the summary I mentioned. I also thought of a solicitor you might enjoy meeting. Shall I introduce you?"

Try it after your next conversation, and check the surveying section of the document for the full wording.
