Written for ecommerce leads weighing a Shopify Plus move, to help them scope the project before they ask agencies for quotes.

Dan NistorVevol Media

Write your Shopify Plus migration brief before you ask any agency for a quote.

Without a brief, each quote prices a slightly different project. One agency assumes order history moves. Another leaves it behind. Then you end up comparing prices for different jobs.

A useful brief answers five questions:

  • What must not break on launch day?
  • Which data moves and which stays?
  • Where does every old URL redirect?
  • Which apps stay, change or go?
  • Who signs off on each decision?

I heard a line on David Senra's Founders Podcast that fits migrations well: "Action produces information."

So before the full move, run a small test. Move a sample of products, customers and orders. You find the problems while they are still small and easy to fix.

The last slide points to a migration brief with two filled-in examples, one for a Magento retailer and one for a WooCommerce brand. Copy the one closest to your store and swap in your own details.

Marketplaces belong in a DTC growth plan, even for brands that love their own Shopify store.

Yasmin Halpin invited me to join a small group visiting Amazon.ie's warehouse in Ireland. The same month, I had ordered The Everything Store by Brad Stone.

Seeing it up close was a reminder that in ecommerce, marketplaces aren't optional. Shoppers search there before they ever find a brand website.

If you run a Shopify brand, here is a simple way to start:

  1. Pick a few products that sell well and ship easily.
  2. Keep price and stock synced from Shopify, so one system stays the source of truth.
  3. Decide who answers marketplace customer questions before the first order arrives.
  4. Read every review and question. They show you what your own product page is missing.
  5. Compare margin after marketplace fees and shipping, product by product.

Start small, learn from real orders, then decide how far to take it.

Worked brief: fashion retailer moving from Magento 2

01About this example

Say a fashion retailer runs Magento 2 with 5,000 products and two storefronts, one for Ireland and one for the UK. They plan to move to Shopify Plus.

This is the brief they would hand to any agency before asking for a quote. Each section answers one question an agency will ask.

Copy it, then swap in your own details line by line. When every quote starts from the same answers, you can compare them line by line.

02What must not break on launch day

  • Checkout in euro for Ireland and in pounds for the UK
  • Customer logins, so returning buyers can see past orders
  • Gift cards and store credit already issued
  • Search traffic to the top category pages
  • Stock sync with the warehouse system

Everything on this list gets tested before launch and checked again on launch day. If an item fails, launch waits.

03Data that moves and data that stays

Moves:

  • 5,000 products with variants, images and size charts
  • Customer accounts for both stores
  • Order history, so customers see past orders in their account
  • Product reviews with dates and star ratings
  • Gift card balances

Stays behind:

  • Abandoned carts
  • Old test products

Customer passwords do not transfer between platforms. Plan an account activation email for launch week and a clear message on the login page.

Owner: the eCommerce manager exports the lists. The agency confirms the format each import needs.

04Redirect plan for old URLs

Magento and Shopify use different URL patterns. Shopify product pages sit under /products/ and categories under /collections/, so almost every old URL changes.

  1. Export every live URL from the old store, including category, product, blog and filter pages.
  2. Pull the pages that bring the most search traffic from Google Search Console and map those first.
  3. Map each old URL to the closest new page. A discontinued product goes to its category, never to the home page.
  4. Load the redirects into Shopify before launch and test a sample by hand.

Example row:
Old: /women/dresses/linen-midi-dress.html
New: /products/linen-midi-dress

05Apps and integrations map

For each tool, write what it does today and what replaces it.

  • Warehouse and stock sync: custom Magento module. Replace with a Shopify Plus integration to the same warehouse system.
  • Size charts: Magento extension. Replace with a product metafield shown on the product page.
  • Loyalty points: check if the current provider has a Shopify app. If yes, move balances. If no, choose a new tool and export balances first.
  • Email marketing: keep the same platform, reconnect it to Shopify and check every signup form.
  • Reviews: export with dates and ratings, then import into a Shopify reviews app.

Mark any item with no clear replacement. Those are the first questions to raise with an agency.

06Test migration before the full move

Move a small sample first: say 100 products across both stores, 500 customer accounts and a batch of past orders.

Then check:

  • Variants, sizes and images match the old store
  • Prices show correctly in euro and pounds
  • A test customer can activate an account and see past orders
  • Redirects for the sample pages land on the right product

Write down every problem the test finds. Each one becomes a task to fix before the full import.

07Launch checks and sign-off

Launch day:

  • Place real test orders in both stores, then refund them
  • Check stock sync with the warehouse after the first orders
  • Crawl the old URL list and confirm each one redirects

After launch:

  • Watch Search Console for pages not found
  • Compare checkout conversion with the old store
  • Collect support tickets about logins or missing orders

Sign-off:

  • eCommerce manager: data, redirects, test orders
  • COO: warehouse and stock sync
  • CMO: design, email flows, loyalty
  • Founder or MD: final go-live date
