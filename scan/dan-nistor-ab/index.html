Before you migrate, write the case on paper

Written for ecommerce leaders at beauty, fragrance and other DTC brands who are comparing platforms. It helps them list what has to carry over in a Shopify Plus migration and what to ask an agency first. The text-only post helps anyone who spends on paid channels check reported returns with a simple regional holdout.

DN
Dan NistorVevol Media - Shopify Plus Agency

One day at Tones of Beauty UK at Excel London with Vevol Media - Shopify Plus Agency, and a lot of good conversations about Shopify, Shopify Plus and migrations.

You can feel the beauty and fragrance categories moving fast. A lot of brands are considering migrating to Shopify.

If you are one of them, here is what I would put on paper before talking to any agency:

  • What has to carry over: customers, order history, reviews
  • Every old URL and where it will go
  • Every integration and who owns it
  • Saved cards and subscriptions
  • A cutover day away from your busiest sales
  • One page that says move or stay, and who signs

Each step is in the slides. The same steps are also laid out as a decision memo with two filled-in examples: a fragrance brand on WooCommerce and a supplement brand on Magento.

Which of these six is hardest for your team to answer today?

Start with what has to survive the move

A migration goes wrong in the parts nobody wrote down. Before you compare agencies, list the data and pages your store cannot lose:

  • Customer accounts and order history
  • Product reviews with ratings and dates
  • Active subscriptions and saved cards
  • Gift cards with a balance
  • Discount codes used in live emails

At the eDesk x Amazon event, the panel said the top sellers answer customers faster than everyone else and never ignore a repeat buyer. A store that loses order history makes both harder.

Dashboard said 1.25x ROAS.
Attribution tool said 4.77x.
Real incremental lift: zero.

That was the slide I told people to read twice when I spent some time in the public data behind AppLovin's push into ecommerce. One brand ran a proper geo holdout test, and the test disagreed with both tools. Results vary by brand, so read it as one case.

It also gives you a cheap way to check your own numbers. If you spend on a paid channel, here is how I would set up a holdout:

  1. Pick two regions with a similar sales history.
  2. Keep the channel running in one and pause it in the other.
  3. Leave everything else alone: email sends, promotions, pricing.
  4. Run it long enough to cover your normal buying cycle.
  5. Compare orders in both regions, then compare the gap with what your dashboard and attribution tool claimed.

If the gap is small next to the reported return, the channel may be taking credit for orders that would have happened anyway. If the gap is large, you now have a number you can defend to your CFO.

Two cautions. Very small regions give noisy results. A big sale in one region will spoil the comparison.

Decision memo: a fragrance brand moves to Shopify Plus

01Why we are looking at this

Say we are a fragrance brand on WooCommerce with 400 product pages, 40 category pages and 60 blog posts. The store runs 10 plugins. Checkout slows down when a campaign brings extra traffic, and every plugin update needs a test order before we trust it.

Leadership needs an answer to one question: does a move to Shopify Plus remove those two problems without losing the search traffic we have built? This memo lists what has to carry over, what could go wrong and who decides.

02What must carry over

  • 30,000 customer accounts with full order history. Repeat buyers are the easiest orders to win, and a buyer who logs in to an empty history contacts support.
  • 10,000 product reviews with star rating, date and reviewer name. Check that the import keeps the ratings, because product pages without reviews can convert differently.
  • Gift cards with a balance. List every live card and its balance before cutover, then confirm the same balance on the new store.
  • Discount codes that appear in live email flows. Recreate each one and test it at checkout.
  • Wishlists. Decide now whether they move or are retired, and tell customers if they are retired.
  • Passwords cannot move between the platforms. Customers get an email to set a new one, so write that message before launch.

03Search and URL plan

We have 500 old URLs: 400 product pages, 40 category pages and 60 blog posts. Each one gets a row in a sheet with three columns: old URL, new URL, page type.

Where the URLs come from: the sitemap, plus the top landing pages from search console and analytics, so nothing that earns traffic is missed.

Rules for the new URL:

  • WooCommerce uses /product/ and Shopify uses /products/, so every product URL changes and needs a redirect. One rule can cover most of them, and we list the exceptions.
  • A retired product goes to its closest category.
  • A category goes to its matching collection. If 20 of the 40 categories are renamed, each rename is written in the sheet with the reason.
  • Blog posts keep their titles and go to the same post under the Shopify blog.
  • The homepage is the last option.

Shopify can import redirects from a CSV file, so this sheet is also the import file. After launch, crawl the 500 old URLs and confirm each one redirects once to a live page, with no chains.

04Integrations and owners

  • Warehouse stock sync. Owner: operations lead. Check that the warehouse system has a Shopify connector, and that stock counts match on the first morning.
  • Email and SMS tool. Owner: CRM lead. Reconnect the store, then test the abandoned cart and post purchase flows with a real order.
  • Reviews. Owner: ecommerce lead. Pick the review app before the import so ratings, dates and photos keep their format.
  • Shipping rules. Owner: operations lead. Rebuild rates and free shipping thresholds for the UK and EU, then place one test order per zone.
  • Payments. Owner: finance director. Confirm every payment method we offer today is available on Shopify Plus for our countries.

For each of the 10 plugins, write one of three answers: replaced by a Shopify feature, replaced by an app, or no longer needed. A plugin with no answer blocks the sign-off.

05Cutover plan

Choose a low-traffic day outside gifting sales and big email sends. Then follow this order:

  1. Build and test on Shopify with a copy of the data.
  2. Freeze product and blog edits on the old store.
  3. Run a final sync of orders and customers.
  4. Switch the domain.
  5. Place a test order with every payment method.
  6. Crawl the 500 old URLs and fix any that do not redirect to a live page.
  7. Send the password message to customers.
  8. Watch search console for new errors and check support inboxes for order history questions.

Each step has a named person who says done in the team channel.

06Decision test

Answer each question yes or no:

  • Can all 500 old URLs redirect to a live page?
  • Do 30,000 accounts and 10,000 reviews import with history and ratings intact?
  • Does each of the 10 plugins have an answer?
  • Does checkout stay fast in a test that copies campaign traffic?
  • Is the cutover day outside gifting sales?

If every answer is yes, recommend the move. If any answer is no, write who fixes it and what needs to be true before we sign.

07Questions for each agency

  1. Who builds the redirect sheet and who checks it after launch?
  2. How do you move 30,000 accounts, and what do customers see on first login?
  3. How do 10,000 reviews import, and what happens to star ratings and dates?
  4. Which of our 10 plugins do you replace with a Shopify feature, and which need an app?
  5. What happens to orders placed during the cutover?
  6. Who watches the store after launch, and how do we reach them?

08One-page summary (draft)

Recommendation: move to Shopify Plus.

Reason: checkout slows under campaign traffic, and plugin updates need a test order every time.

Conditions: all 500 old URLs redirect to a live page. 30,000 accounts and 10,000 reviews import with history and ratings intact. Each of the 10 plugins has an answer.

Cutover: a low-traffic day outside gifting sales, with a final data sync and a test order on every payment method.

Sign-off: the head of ecommerce recommends, the finance director approves and the operations lead confirms the stock sync.

How to build the redirect sheet from your migration memo

DNDan Nistorto Alex

Hi Sam,

Here is one detail from section three of the memo, because it is the one people tend to rush: the redirect sheet.

In the fragrance example the store has 400 product pages, 40 category pages and 60 blog posts. That makes 500 old URLs. Every one of them needs a row.

How to fill it in:

  1. Export your URLs from the sitemap. Then add your top landing pages from search console and analytics, in case something earns traffic but is missing from the sitemap.
  2. Use three columns: old URL, new URL, page type.
  3. Map products to the same product. WooCommerce uses /product/ and Shopify uses /products/, so every product URL changes and every one needs a redirect. A single rule can cover most of them, and you list the exceptions.
  4. Map retired products to the closest category, and categories to their matching collection.
  5. Send a page to the homepage only when nothing else fits.
  6. Avoid chains. Each old URL should redirect once, straight to a live page.

Shopify lets you import redirects from a CSV file, so this sheet can be the import file. After launch, crawl the old list and check that all 500 return a redirect to a page that loads.

If you want to compare your own counts with the example, reply with your product and category page numbers and I will tell you where I would look first.
