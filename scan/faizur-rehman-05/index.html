A month after I hand off a design, I check in to see how it's performing.

Most designers disappear the moment the invoice is paid. That's normal, the project is done and there's another client waiting. But I follow up a month later to see how the work is performing, mostly to see what's actually happening once real users touch it.

If you're working with a designer on your app right now, ask for that check-in yourself instead of waiting for them to offer it. A signed-off Figma file won't tell you if your onboarding flow is confusing people or if the redesign changed how they use the product. That shows up later, once someone looks at it again after people have actually used it for a few weeks.

That conversation a month out is usually where I learn if the thing I built is actually working, or just looks finished.

When a client asks how to keep working with me after a project ships, I give them one rule up front: no new screens, no new features. What I'll do instead is refine the existing design, work through use cases we didn't think of before launch, and sit with the developers when something doesn't translate from Figma to code.

For founders who need more than a one-off project, I usually offer something like 20 hours a month at $5k instead of billing by the hour. Some weeks that's a five-minute tweak to a button state someone on the team keeps flagging. Other weeks it's two full days rethinking a flow that isn't converting.

New screens and new features are a separate project with their own scope. The 20 hours are for making what already shipped actually hold up once real users are in it.

A client once asked me how we could keep working together once the project wrapped.

Until that question, I hadn't planned for it. The project would end, I'd invoice, and whatever came next was a new conversation with no shape to it. So I built one. 20 hours a month for $5k, and inside that block I do one kind of work only, refining the design that already exists and working through use cases with the team, while staying available to the developers when something doesn't translate cleanly from file to build.

New screens and new features don't fit inside that block. Those get scoped and priced as their own project, and I tell clients that upfront.

If you're a founder who found a designer you actually want to keep, ask for that shape. A monthly hour allowance for refinement and support gives both sides something to plan around. An open-ended "can you also" request usually doesn't, and most designers will eventually start saying no to those too.

A few one-time app design jobs turned into exactly this kind of monthly arrangement for me, and I still work with two of those clients now.
