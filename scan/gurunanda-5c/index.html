What we’d test first

01

Test a Welcome Kit offer on the Farmed Mint oil pulling page

The Farmed Mint oil pulling page sampled shows a Perfect Additions row with individual products. Your bundles page offers a customizable Welcome Kit with one oil pulling product, one toothpaste and one mouthwash.

Proposed test

Variant A keeps the current Perfect Additions row. Variant B replaces it with one module: "Make it a routine: pick your oil, toothpaste and mouthwash in one Welcome Kit", linking to the kit builder. Split visitors to this page 50/50. Kit contents and any discount stay as shown on the kit page.

How we’d measure it

Revenue per visitor from completed purchases in both arms. Guardrails: average order value and refund rate. Module clicks are a diagnostic only, not the winning metric.

02

Show weeks of daily use beside the oil pulling kit pack options

The Coconut & Peppermint starter kit page offers a 1 Pack and 2 Pack selector. Your page also states that a bottle lasts about 4 weeks at 10-15ml per daily session, but the selector itself is labelled only by count.

Proposed test

Keep the current pack labels in the control. Add "about 4 weeks of daily use" beside 1 Pack and "about 8 weeks of daily use" beside 2 Pack in the variant, based on your page's stated bottle life. Keep prices, products and layout the same. Split visitors evenly.

How we’d measure it

Revenue per visitor from completed purchases in both arms. Guardrails: refund rate and return requests. Share of 2 Pack orders is a diagnostic only.

03

Test the mess-free gel pen benefit in the whitening kit ad

The sampled Meta ad leads with a get-ready-time moment and sends traffic to the Wireless LED Teeth Whitening Kit page. That page states the kit includes a gel pen with no drips or trays, a benefit the ad does not lead with.

Proposed test

Keep the current get-ready-time ad in the control. Test a headline about the mess-free gel pen in the variant: "Whiten with a mess-free gel pen and a wireless LED kit." Keep the body, image, audience and landing page unchanged. Compare both headlines with an even traffic split.

How we’d measure it

Winning metric: kit purchases per ad click, with revenue per visitor as a tiebreaker. Guardrail: refund and return requests on kit orders. Add-to-cart rate is a diagnostic only.

Finding 1 · Shopper paths

Free shipping starts at $50, and your typical product costs $9.99

A shopper buying one typical item at $9.99 pays for delivery unless they add more to reach $50.

Recommended next step

Compare the current $50 free-shipping threshold with a $30 threshold, splitting eligible US visitors evenly. Keep product prices and other offers unchanged. Measure contribution profit per visitor after product, shipping and fulfilment costs. Track purchase conversion and average order value as guardrails. Confirm the shipping subsidy fits your margins before running the test.
