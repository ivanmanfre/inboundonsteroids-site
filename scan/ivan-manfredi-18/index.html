Your sales calls are the easiest source of LinkedIn posts you have.

An agency owner told me she had nothing to post about. She had been staring at a blank composer for weeks.

Meanwhile three months of her sales calls sat in a Fathom folder, transcribed and searchable. We read back through them and counted something like 30 to 40 posts worth of material.

She had never once opened that folder to write from. Nobody had run a pass over the transcripts.

Here is what to pull from each call:

  • The objection and the answer that moved the deal
  • The client's problem, said back sharper than any headline
  • A client telling you what the work changed
  • The question prospects keep asking

A marketing guru once looked at a client feed I run and asked if it was a real case study. Those were real lines from that founder's sales calls.

02Lead magnets

Turn readers into a list you own.

For agency founders who record sales or client calls and want a worked method for turning those transcripts into LinkedIn posts that sound like them.

Ivan ManfrediIvan Manfredi

A lot of agency founders already record the calls that could fill their feed. What's usually missing is a way to read those calls for posts.

So I put together the Call-to-Post Guide.

It is a worked document for pulling LinkedIn posts out of call transcripts, built around two use cases:

  1. Your own sales calls. An example from a paid media agency's sales call: the price objection, the answer, the filled-in pull note and the finished post draft.
  2. Your team's client calls. For founders who only sit on some calls now, with an example of turning an account lead's line into an "our team found" post.

Each example shows the transcript line, what to keep, what to remove before publishing and how it reads once it is a post.

It also covers what to look for on every pass: objections and the answers that landed, the client's problem in their own words, results a client says out loud and the questions prospects keep asking.

If you have a folder of recordings you have never written from, start with the call where a prospect pushed back.
