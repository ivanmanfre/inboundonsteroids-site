Where we’d focus

What we’d test first

01

Test set-specific ad copy against broad copy

Your two active Meta ads say "Discover your haircare essentials, from nourishing shampoo and hair masks to targeted scalp care and hair growth support" and invite people to "explore the collection", and the link recorded for both is the Follicle Defence Complex set page. One is a video ad and one uses Meta's dynamic format. That page lists the set at £60.00 and describes it as a four-step system. So the ads invite people to browse a range, but the click lands on one £60.00 set. Copy that names the set and its price before the click may bring in people who are already expecting that page.

Proposed test

Run this on your video ad, whose recorded link is the set page. Keep that link and change only the ad message. Leave the dynamic ad out of this test, since it may rotate products. Variant draft: "One four-step routine for scalp and hair care. Shop the Follicle Defence Complex, £60.00." Use the price line only in UK-targeted ad sets. Run it against the current broad copy, with equal budget and the same audience and dates.

How we’d measure it

Winning metric: cost per purchase at equal spend, same audience and dates. Guardrails: purchases per click on the set page and average order value. Early read: click-through and set-page add-to-cart rates per variant, until purchases are enough to compare.

02

Test a £10 travel kit option on the full set page

Your set page sells the four-step routine at £60.00. Your travel kit page offers the same four steps for £10.00, for trying products before committing to full sizes, and the set page links to it only lower down.

Proposed test

On the set page, add a short line beside Add to Cart: "Not ready for the full set? Try the four-step Travel Kit first." It links to the travel kit page. Compare against the current page.

How we’d measure it

Revenue per set-page visitor, counting set and travel kit orders in both arms. Guardrail: full set purchases and average order value.

03

Offer the hair growth serum as an add-on on the set page

Your set page says the serum is "purchased separately as an optional fifth step". Your collection page lists the serum at £35.00. Your bundles page sells all five steps, serum included, as a monthly subscription. But on the set page as we read it, we saw no way for a one-off buyer to add the serum. Your homepage says of the serum: "The heart of the Lushka system". Your ads mention hair growth support, and the set page they link to sells the four steps without the serum.

Proposed test

On the set page, add an unticked option below the quantity selector: "Add the Multi-Peptide Hair Growth Serum, the optional fifth step." It adds the serum to the same cart as the set. Compare against the page with no add-on option.

How we’d measure it

First-order revenue per set-page visitor, same in both arms. Guardrail: set add-to-cart and checkout completion rates.
