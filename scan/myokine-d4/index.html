01 / Proposed paid test

Test gym-bag versus daily-use ad message

The three live Meta ads we read share one text, "We Sold Out!", with a restock sign-up, while your product page takes pre-orders shipping from 16 November. Your reviews echo both the page's gym-bag reset and the FAQ's "Daily use is completely fine", so it is unresolved which reason sells better to ad visitors once those ads are replaced.

One change

Proposed new Meta ad test, not an observed ad. Two ads, same product page, only the message differs. A: "No time to shower? Keep Myokine Refresh in your gym bag and mist after training." B: "Make Myokine Refresh your daily skin mist, workout or not." Launch once the page shows in stock and end both with "Order now".

02 / Your store

Test an after-training and evening headline

Your product page opens by describing Refresh as a post-workout spray, so it is unresolved whether a broader daily message helps shoppers decide. The page already has an evening-use section lower down, and your FAQ confirms use on non-training days.

One change

Proposed page test: show half of product-page visitors the headline "Your skin's cool-down, after training and before bed", with a line covering gym-bag and evening use. The other half keep the current post-workout-only lead.

What we saw
Your product page opens by describing Refresh as a post-workout spray, so it is unresolved whether a broader daily message helps shoppers decide. The page already has an evening-use section lower down, and your FAQ confirms use on non-training days.

What to measure
Purchases per product-page visitor, split evenly at random for at least two weeks while stock status stays unchanged. Run it before or after the ad test. Check by traffic source.
