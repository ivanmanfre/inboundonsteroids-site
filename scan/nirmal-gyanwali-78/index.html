01Posts

Reach marketing leads turning sales insight into website pages.

Written for marketing leaders who want their website to answer the questions buyers already ask sales before the first call.

Written for
  • B2B marketing leaders
  • eCommerce founders and marketers
  • Marketing managers in regulated industries
Nirmal GyanwaliWP Creative

Your sales team hears what your website should say every day.

The trouble is that marketing rarely hears it.

We reviewed recorded sales conversations for a client recently. The same questions kept coming up, with the same concerns and the same hesitation. A lot of it surfaced well before anyone talked about price.

Meanwhile, most marketing teams update landing pages and FAQs from personas, keyword research and analytics. All useful. None of them carry the words buyers use when they are deciding whether to go ahead.

A simple way to close that gap:

  • Collect the questions from recent recorded calls.
  • Write them down in the buyer's own words.
  • Sort each one by the page that should answer it.
  • Rewrite that page, then tell sales what changed.

For long B2B sales cycles this matters even more. People come back to the site several times before a decision, and each visit is a chance to answer the question they did not feel comfortable asking on a call.

The slides walk through each step. The last one points to a worked brief with two filled-in examples you can copy.

02Lead magnets

Turn readers into a list you own.

For marketing managers who want to turn recorded sales questions into specific page changes, with two filled-in examples to copy from.

Nirmal GyanwaliWP Creative

If your sales calls are recorded, you already have the raw material for your next website update.

The hard part is turning a pile of recordings into page changes someone can actually ship.

This worked brief shows how, start to finish, using two example businesses:

  • A B2B IT services firm with a long sales cycle and several people in every decision.
  • An online mattress store where questions come through phone orders and chat.

For each one you get:

  1. A filled-in question log, written in the buyer's words.
  2. A map of which page should answer each question.
  3. Before and after copy for the pages that change.
  4. What goes back to the sales or service team once the page is live.

It is written for marketing managers who own the website but do not sit in on sales calls.

Use it as a model, swap in your own questions, and hand the finished page list to whoever runs your site.
