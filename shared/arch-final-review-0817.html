3. A campaign of ten

DS
Davorin SmitFounder, ARCH · Creator-led UA for games & apps1d · 🌐
10 influencers. That is the whole creator roster on one of the campaigns we are proudest of. Incogni, 5 countries, 20 pieces of content. It sits on our site next to campaigns fifty times the size. A number that small usually reads as a test, or as a client who ran out of money halfway. Incogni sells data removal. The people who buy it are already worried about their data before anyone talks to them, and there are maybe a dozen creators in Europe who can talk about that without sounding like an advert. We went and got 10 of them. Adding 30 more names would have bought us numbers we did not need, from people the audience would not have believed. 10 was the answer to the brief.

4. The four things, and nothing else

DS
Davorin SmitFounder, ARCH · Creator-led UA for games & apps1d · 🌐
We do not report on awareness. Our page says what we are graded on and it is four words long. Installs, CPI, ROAS, retention. There is no fifth box where sentiment goes. The reason is that we cannot move it on purpose. A creator campaign changes how many people install and what they do on day seven. Whether a market feels warmer about a brand afterwards is real, and I have never been able to book against it. So we sell the four and stay out of the rest. It is a narrower promise than most agencies make. Fine by me.

6. Five years of this

DS
Davorin SmitFounder, ARCH · Creator-led UA for games & apps1d · 🌐
5 years, over 5,000 creators, 5 continents. Those are the three numbers on the front of our website and I want to say the unfashionable thing that sits under them. Most of what we sell is booking. Availability, price, dates, rights. 5,000 creator relationships. 15 countries on one VPN campaign. The part of this job that decides whether a campaign works is whether the right person said yes, on a date, at a price, with the rights written down. Everything upstream of that, the concepts, the hooks, the trend reads, matters far less than the industry pretends. You can have a brilliant idea and no one available in October. Boring, and true. If creative is what you do, this is an annoying thing to read from an agency. The brief still shapes who is even worth asking, and a bad brief wastes the best roster in Europe. We have run campaigns where the concept did the heavy lifting. Surfshark was 500 creators across 15 countries, and a VPN is not a game. Nothing about running a VPN campaign resembles running one for a mobile game except the part where you find out who will actually say yes. So, still the unfashionable version, just smaller: mostly, this is booking.

7. One city, thirty countries

DS
Davorin SmitFounder, ARCH · Creator-led UA for games & apps1d · 🌐
Hey. Someone asked me last week which local agencies we use in each market. Fair question, it's how most of this gets done. We do not use any. The campaigns run in thirty countries. The people running them sit in Zagreb, in one office, and between them they work in ten languages. When we hire a campaign manager the ad usually says which language we're short of. German, French, Italian, Spanish. I understand why the local-partner model exists. Somebody on the ground knows which creator is having a moment in Poland this month, and we've been wrong about that before. What we wouldn't give up is the thing you only get in one room. A campaign manager in Zagreb can turn around and ask the one who ran the same genre in Italy last quarter what the going rate was. That conversation doesn't happen across three agencies and four contracts, and it's most of what stops a client overpaying. The honest version of our answer: no local partners, and we buy that back with languages and a shared room. Would you take that trade in your market, or is there something local you wouldn't hand over?

8. One bad video from your calls

DS
Davorin SmitFounder, ARCH · Creator-led UA for games & apps1d · 🌐
The most overpriced creator in any market is the one with a clean record. Everyone books the same short list of names that have never flopped, and pays what that scarcity costs. Meanwhile: we watched a big Polish creator grow like crazy on one video. The next one flopped, a string of unlucky circumstances, and he disappeared from every roster. Nobody has called him since. The guy can obviously perform. The numbers from the video before say so. One flop is one data point. There is a pile of creators sitting in that spot. Proven once, unlucky once, priced like a risk. That is where incentive deals go. Smaller fee, bonus per install past a threshold. If the flop was bad luck, the second video proves it, and proves it cheap. If it was not, the miss costs almost nothing.

9. Same creator, two contracts from your calls

DS
Davorin SmitFounder, ARCH · Creator-led UA for games & apps1d · 🌐
We are changing how some creator deals are paid. The shape: a smaller flat fee, plus a CPI bonus once installs pass a threshold. What pushed us there is one creator on our roster. On a campaign where his pay tracked installs, he pushed the content himself and the install numbers were absurd. On a one-video flat deal later, the result came in under expectations. Same person. Same audience. Different contract. A flat fee buys a video. The bonus buys what he does after the video is up. The part we have not settled is where the threshold sits. Too low and it is just a fee with extra paperwork. Too high and nobody believes the bonus is real.

10. In Serbia, it is Viber from your calls

DS
Davorin SmitFounder, ARCH · Creator-led UA for games & apps1d · 🌐
Every playbook says the same thing about creator communities. WhatsApp groups, broadcast channels, that is where the untapped reach sits. We started checking our own markets. In the Balkans, WhatsApp communities mostly do not exist. The communities are on Discord, on Facebook, sometimes Telegram. Instagram broadcast channels, yes. And in Serbia the number one chat app is Viber. Some of our creators have run Viber groups. An install plan that says "WhatsApp communities" was written for a different continent. Before you budget a geo, ask where people in that country actually message each other, and rebuild the channel list from the answer. Nobody sends us a brief that starts there.
