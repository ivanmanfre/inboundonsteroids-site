I hate it when an agency hands the client a list of 1,000 creators and calls that the work. Here's the list. Review them all. Tell us who you want. That review is the job. It's the part you're paying for. So we do it before anything reaches a client now. Every row carries the previous brand partnerships, how many of those renewed, the CPM, and how far that CPM swings month to month. Slower to build. Shorter lists. A client told us what that changes on his side: more points to review per name, so he decides on our list with confidence instead of going off to redo the research himself. If you run an agency and your sheet is names and links, you're sending your homework to the customer.

The best influencer campaigns we've run share one trait: everything went live on the same day. In Germany we once launched 30 creators on a single date. Hell of a job. Insane pace. We managed it, and it was one of our best campaigns. The reason it works is boring and mechanical. 30 videos landing together push your installs into a 48-hour spike. The store notices. Organic picks up and your paid campaigns ride the same wave. Spread those same 30 videos out week by week and you get 30 small bumps no store algorithm will ever care about.

Post 5BuyerFrom your call with Domas, 19 Aug

Davorin SmitFounder, ARCH · Creator-led UA for games & apps1d · 🌐
···
An influencer marketing lead I work with approves creators in 2 passes. Pass 1 is content only: does the channel fit the game. This week a channel cleared that pass. He expected the rate to land near $800. The ask came back at $4,000. 5x, on a creator he already wanted. Quarterly creator plans die on exactly this. The plan commits to a number of videos, the number was built on guessed rates, and nobody rebuilds it once the real asks arrive. We've watched a quarter's video count drop to a fraction of the plan on an unchanged budget. Nothing was overspent. The rates were guessed too low before the quarter started. If you're building next quarter's creator plan, message 10 channels you'd genuinely book and collect 10 real rates first. Build the video count on those.

Post 6TrustFrom your HOLO call, 17 Aug

Davorin SmitFounder, ARCH · Creator-led UA for games & apps1d · 🌐
···
Your organic install number is probably lying to you, and it's flattering you while it does it. Here's the mechanic. A creator posts. Someone watches, doesn't tap the link, opens the store an hour later and searches your game by name. That install lands in organic. You look at the report, see organic climbing, and conclude your brand is finally compounding. So you cut creator spend, because organic is free and creators aren't. Then organic falls, and nobody can explain why. We put coupon codes and QR on campaigns for exactly this, so the install has somewhere to go that isn't the organic bucket. Do this before your next campaign. Write down your organic baseline for the 2 weeks before anything goes live. Then watch what organic does in the 2 weeks after. If that line tracks your creator calendar, those installs were theirs and you have been paying for them twice. Move them into the campaign column and re-run your cost per install with them counted.

Post 7TrustFrom your call with Domas, 19 Aug

Davorin SmitFounder, ARCH · Creator-led UA for games & apps1d · 🌐
···
A client asked me to find him fewer creators this quarter. On purpose. His reasoning (and I think he's right): - Once a channel works, the cheapest video you'll ever buy is the 2nd one - You know the audience converts and you know the rate - A new name costs you all of that again, and you still don't know if it performs. So the plan moved from a long list of new channels to a short list we go deeper on. Monthly slot, dedicated video, review format, whatever that creator is actually good at. And the 2nd negotiation is easier. The relationship is already there, and both sides know what the last deal looked like. What I can't tell you yet is where the ceiling sits. At some point an audience gets tired of hearing about the same product and the returns fall over. Still learning where that point is.
