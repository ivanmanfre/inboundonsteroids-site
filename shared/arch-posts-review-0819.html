I hate it when an agency hands the client a list of 1,000 creators and calls that the work. Here's the list. Review them all. Tell us who you want. That review is the job. It's the part you're paying for. So we do it before anything reaches a client now. Post 3TrustFrom your Candivore bi-weekly, 20 Aug

Davorin SmitFounder, ARCH · Creator-led UA for games & apps1d · 🌐
···
The best campaigns we've run share one trait: everything went live on the same day. In Germany we once launched 30 creators on a single date. Hell of a job. Insane pace. We managed it, and I still measure campaigns against that one. The reason it works is boring and mechanical. 30 videos landing together push your installs into a 48-hour spike. The store notices. Ranking climbs, organic search picks up, and your paid campaigns ride the same wave. Spread those same 30 videos out week by week and you get 30 small bumps no store algorithm will ever care about. The price is discipline. You lock the date a month out. First drafts arrive 7 days before it, final cuts 3 days before. Some creators will drop. Someone always can't post that day. So you're re-approving a replacement in 48 hours, because the date doesn't move. That sprint is the part teams quietly skip. The date slips a week for one creator, then another, and the spike you planned turns back into a drip. If your launches trickle out one creator at a time, batch the next one. Even 2 groups of 10 beats 20 solo release dates.

Post 4BuyerFrom your call with Domas, 19 Aug

Davorin SmitFounder, ARCH · Creator-led UA for games & apps1d · 🌐

You budget the quarter at the rate you expect. Then the asks come back. A buyer I work with put a number on that this week. He looks at a channel, decides it's a fit, expects the ask to land near $800. He gets $4,000. 5x. On a channel he'd already approved on content alone. That gap is where content plans quietly die. The deck commits to a number of videos for the quarter. The number was built on an assumed rate. Nobody revisits it once the first replies land. We've watched a quarter's unit count drop to a fraction of the plan on an unchanged budget, purely because the asks came back where the market actually sits. Nothing was overspent. The rate assumption was wrong, and it was wrong before the quarter started. You can price this properly in a week. Send 10 real outreach messages to channels you'd genuinely book, and collect 10 real asks. That's your rate. Build the plan on that one.

Post 5TrustFrom your HOLO call, 17 Aug

Davorin SmitFounder, ARCH · Creator-led UA for games & apps1d · 🌐
···
Your organic install number is probably lying to you, and it's flattering you while it does it. Here's the mechanic. A creator posts. Someone watches, doesn't tap the link, opens the store an hour later and searches your game by name. That install lands in organic. You look at the report, see organic climbing, and conclude your brand is finally compounding. So you cut creator spend, because organic is free and creators aren't. Then organic falls, and nobody can explain why. We put coupon codes and QR on campaigns for exactly this. Not for the discount. So the install has somewhere to go that isn't the organic bucket. Do this before your next campaign. Write down your organic baseline for the 2 weeks before anything goes live. Then watch what organic does in the 2 weeks after. If that line tracks your creator calendar, those installs were theirs and you have been paying for them twice. Move them into the campaign column and re-run your cost per install with them counted.

Post 6TrustFrom your call with Domas, 19 Aug

Davorin SmitFounder, ARCH · Creator-led UA for games & apps1d · 🌐

A client asked me to find him fewer creators this quarter. On purpose. His reasoning, and I think he's right. Once a channel works, the cheapest video you'll ever buy is the 2nd one from that same channel. You already know the audience converts. You already have the rate. You've already had the awkward conversation about usage rights and posting windows. A new name costs you all 3 again, and you still don't know if it performs. So the plan moved from a long list of new channels to a short list we go deeper on. Monthly slot, dedicated video, review format, whatever that creator is actually good at. The part I didn't expect is that the 2nd negotiation is easier. They've been paid, they were paid on time, and they know what working with us feels like. What I can't tell you yet is where the ceiling sits. At some point an audience gets tired of hearing about the same product and the returns fall over. I don't know where that point is. Before you open a new sourcing list next quarter, go price a 2nd booking with everyone who already performed.

It stayed on the roadmap because it was on the roadmap. I see the same thing in plans people send me. A geo earns its slot once, in a kickoff deck, and then nobody re-checks it. Move the money to a cheaper country you've never tried, and for the same spend you learn something you didn't know. Keeping a dead geo funded is the most polite way I know to lose a quarter.
