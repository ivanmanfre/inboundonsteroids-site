DS
Davorin SmitFounder, ARCH · Creator-led UA for games & apps1d · 🌐
We do not send a client the same video twice. This is how we run it with one of our biggest clients. All feedback goes onto the draft in one pass, every change written as a comment, and the creator updates the video once. The client sees the video again when it is the final version. Narrower than "unlimited revisions", on purpose. Every extra round adds days and changes little. You may disagree with this one. All good.

Post 3 of 6 Two Slack threads

From your internal call, 17 Aug, the two Slack threads. Point: the state of a campaign readable in one scroll, without status meetings.

DS
Davorin SmitFounder, ARCH · Creator-led UA for games & apps1d · 🌐
I made two Slack threads for one campaign, honestly just to make my own life easier. Thread one is "pitches". When a creator is ready to pitch, the team drops one comment with the name, it goes into the sheet, and the client contact gets pinged. Thread two is "date confirmations". When a creator accepts the offer and confirms a date, one comment again, and contract signing starts on our side. That's the whole system! No dashboard, no tool, nothing to "implement". The reason it works is boring. Both threads carry events only, one comment per event, and every discussion lives somewhere else. So scrolling one thread tells you the exact state of the campaign, which no status meeting ever managed for me. What's changed from day one is visibility. You can see who's actually moving without asking anyone.

Post 4 of 6 A quiet inbox

From your internal call, 17 Aug. No numbers, no client, no deadline in the post. Point: when replies drop, fix the sending side before blaming the platform.

DS
Davorin SmitFounder, ARCH · Creator-led UA for games & apps1d · 🌐
Creator replies came in low this month, and the miss was ours. Mid-campaign, no platform drama in it. Our outreach simply assumed a reply rate that didn't show up. The fix is on our side. When replies drop, the sending side has to grow. So we pressed on scouting, widened the list past the first obvious categories, and tightened the follow-ups in between. To be honest, none of this is clever, and there's no way around it that we know of. If the rate stays low after the volume doubles, then the list itself is wrong, and that's a different, harder problem. If your replies dropped this summer too, look at the sending side first.

Post 5 of 6 The check has an expiry date

From your internal call, 17 Aug, the every-video check on new campaigns. Point: the senior check is deliberate and temporary, it expires once both sides read quality the same way.

DS
Davorin SmitFounder, ARCH · Creator-led UA for games & apps1d · 🌐
When a campaign is new, someone senior on our side watches every creator video before the client sees a frame. From outside that looks like an agency bottleneck. Fair. It does not scale. It slows the first weeks down. We run it anyway, with one difference. The check has an expiry date. Every video gets reviewed for roughly the first two months, while we and the client are still aligning on what "good" means for them. After that the check is gone by design, because keeping it longer would mean the alignment never happened. Your interest in speed is right. Full speed from day one just costs you the alignment, and you pay for that later, in revisions.

Post 6 of 6 Hours, mostly

From your internal call, 17 Aug, scouting platforms against manual work. No tool named. Point: finding creators is hours and criteria, the software is the small part.

DS
Davorin SmitFounder, ARCH · Creator-led UA for games & apps1d · 🌐
800 names, give or take, sit on one scouting list of ours, so take this as experience talking: manual scouting finds better creators than the platforms do. About 800 in the sheet. Fifty-ish from very little trying, by hand. Every discovery tool we've paid for taught us the same thing, and this isn't a guess. The software is the limited part of it. Hours, mostly. I searched one of our categories on YouTube myself a while back, like a viewer would, and my own suggested feed has been showing me herds of creators who fit ever since. Nobody configured a scouting tool to do that. I just looked at the category the way the audience does. No offence to the platform teams, truly. The bases are real, the biggest one we've used is genuinely deep, and we still run searches there. It's the quick part of a slow job.
