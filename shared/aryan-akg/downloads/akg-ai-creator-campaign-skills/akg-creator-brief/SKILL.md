# Creator brief writer

Use this after a product and creator have a plausible fit, or when a team needs a draft brief to discuss with candidates. Preserve creator voice while making the product demonstration and measurement clear.

## Collect the brief inputs

Ask for the product link or approved fact sheet, target user and market, campaign objective, one demonstrable task and the result it produces. Also request platform, creator, format, deliverables, timeline, approved claims, proof or screenshots, required CTA, tracked link or code, review owner, disclosures and any restrictions. Ask about usage rights, paid amplification and exclusivity if those matter to the deal.

If a fact or term is missing, leave a labelled **To confirm** field. Never invent product capabilities, customer outcomes, legal claims, discount terms, creator fees or rights. A brief can be drafted with gaps; it cannot be treated as ready to send until the owner approves them.

## Build the brief

1. Open with the audience problem and one clear viewing outcome. State why this creator's audience might care, using supplied evidence only.
2. Write a 20-30 second demonstration sequence: starting task, on-screen action, visible result and CTA. For a complex B2B tool, narrow the sequence to one recognisable job rather than trying to explain the whole platform.
3. Give the creator room to choose language and filming style. Separate **Must show or say** from **Creative options**. Make each mandatory element attributable to a user-supplied requirement.
4. List deliverables and operational details: format and length, draft date, review turnaround, live date, links or tags, disclosure, files to deliver, reporting date, rights and payment terms. Mark unknown terms **To confirm**.
5. Pair the goal with a measurement plan. Specify the source for views, clicks, sign-ups or other outcomes, the window, and who collects it. Do not promise results.

## Return

A one-page brief with these headings: Campaign objective; Audience and use case; The demo; Must include; Creative freedom; Deliverables and dates; CTA and tracking; Review and reporting; Terms to confirm. Finish with a short checklist of missing approvals that prevents this draft from being sent as a final brief.

Use straightforward language a creator can act on. Do not turn an unverified product claim into a script line.
