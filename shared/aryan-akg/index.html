Expertise · product fit

Aryan Gulzar · YouFounder, AKG MediaDraft for review
in
When we look at an AI product for a creator campaign, I want to understand what it does within about 30 seconds. The person watching needs to get it that quickly too. Some products make that easy. You can show a task, use the product and put the result on screen. A video tool gives you a video. A presentation tool gives you a deck someone can use. For a more technical product, the brief needs extra work. We need a specific audience who understands the problem, and a use case they can recognise in their own work. That applies to B2B as well. The creator has to reach the people who could use it. Before choosing creators, I'd write down the task the viewer should see and the reason they'd try the product afterwards. If that takes several paragraphs to explain, I'd work on the demonstration before booking a placement.

A creator campaign puts a lot of work behind one published video. The brief needs to be usable. Someone has to agree the rate, check the content, manage changes and keep the go-live date moving. Then the team needs to track what happened after it went out. We've managed 150+ creators across 10+ campaigns at the same time. At that volume, every person needs to know what they're responsible for and what happens next. We build internal tools for campaign tracking, payments and operations because those jobs keep coming back. If you're growing an influencer programme, look at how the next campaign gets delivered. Who owns the review? Where does a delayed video show up? Who checks the result? Those are useful questions to ask an agency before you commit the budget.

Buyer · campaign measurement

Aryan Gulzar · YouFounder, AKG MediaDraft for review
in
Before a creator campaign starts, the team needs to agree what success would look like. Is the goal product awareness, attributed sign-ups or customers at an acquisition cost the business can support? That answer affects the creators we shortlist and the content we ask them to make. It also affects the tracking we need before anything goes live. A tutorial can explain a product in detail. A short demonstration can introduce one useful feature. The choice should follow the product, the audience and the job of the campaign. At AKG, we track placements against the goal agreed at the start, including sign-ups and CPA where attribution allows. For your next brief, write down the result you're trying to measure and how you'll recognise it. The shortlist will be easier to assess with that in front of you.

A creator shortlist is easier to review when everyone is looking at the same questions. Does the audience include the people who could use your product? Is there a clear use case to demonstrate? What do comparable sponsored placements tell you? Then there are the commercial details. The placement price, what you get permission to reuse, when the video goes live and how you'll track the result.
