We recorded a podcast with Karl O'Brien a few months back and one of the topics on the table was why brands hate agencies, and where those relationships actually fall apart. I run an agency. A lot of my friends run agencies. So I asked him where the hate comes from. Karl's answer was the most useful thing I've heard on it. The agency is often the one putting the ad spend to use, and he put that at anywhere between 10 and 40% of revenue. The founder wants to talk about the financial side of the business. The agency, in his words, is "spitting marketing metrics back at them". I've been that agency. A few years ago a client of ours did about half a million in sales over Black Friday. When we reviewed it in January the margins were gone. We had no sight of their costs, and we had never asked for them. So if you're about to hire an agency, or replace one, I'd start with one question. Have they asked to see your costs? The full conversation with Karl is up on YouTube, I'll drop the link in the comments.

For years I thought my job was to improve ROAS. I came into every brand with ideas to push that number up, and in all fairness, pre-COVID most of us were in that boat. ROAS tells you how an ad performs today. CAC to LTV tells you whether the business can scale for years. So now the first thing we map for a new brand is LTV over time, with the true CAC beside it. True CAC means everything it took to get that customer: • ad spend • creator fees • agency fees • content • the smaller tools running in the background Let's say two brands both pay €40 to acquire a customer. The first gets €55 back on day 0 and €110 by day 90. That brand can usually scale hard. The second gets €30 on day 0 and €45 by day 90. That brand is bleeding cash while it waits for retention to save it. Same CAC. It won't hold for everyone. If you sell sofas, nobody is buying four a year. I broke the whole thing down in the video we now use as the SOP for every new client.

If you turned your ads off for the next 30 days, what happens to the business? Imagine two brands. Both do €100k a month. From the outside they look identical. Under the hood: Brand A gets 90% of that from new customers every month. Brand B gets 40% of it from customers coming back. Which one survives a bad ad month? Which one can scale without panic? Acquisition matters. Totally get that. It's mainly what I built my career on. That momentum is fragile though. It's strange to build a whole system around something you don't really control. When customers start coming back on their own, a few things change. you stop rebuilding revenue from zero every month cash flow gets more predictable the ad account gets a lot less stressful and you usually have more cash in the bank It can be boring work. It probably builds the strongest businesses I see.
